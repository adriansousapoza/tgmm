    GPCM Covariance Models¶

    GaussianMixture supports all 14 Celeux & Govaert (1995) Gaussian Parsimonious Clustering Models (GPCM) -- the same family mclust implements -- not just the six classic covariance types in the main Gaussian Mixture Model guide. This page defines the models, states the M-step each one uses and why it is correct, and lists what is and isn't supported. The Covariance Structures tutorial walks through the same material with runnable code, figures, and a numerical check of every formula below.

    Where the covariance enters EM¶

    Given responsibilities \(r_{ik}\) from the E-step, write

    \[ n_k=\sum_{i} r_{ik},\qquad \bar y_k=\frac1{n_k}\sum_i r_{ik}x_i,\qquad W_k=\sum_{i} r_{ik}\,(x_i-\bar y_k)(x_i-\bar y_k)^{\top}. \]

    After maximising the expected complete-data log-likelihood over \(\pi_k\) and \(\mu_k\), the only terms left that depend on the covariances are, up to a factor \(-\tfrac12\),

    \[ J(\Sigma_1,\dots,\Sigma_K)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\Big[\,n_k\log|\Sigma_k|+\operatorname{tr}\big(\Sigma_k^{-1}W_k\big)\Big]. \]

    Every covariance type is the same objective \(J\) minimised over a different feasible set, and the M-step needs the data only through \((n_k, W_k)\) (the SufficientStats.nk and .s2 fields). For full the minimiser is \(\Sigma_k=W_k/n_k\); the other classic types are the restrictions listed in the Gaussian Mixture Model guide. reg_covar is added to the diagonal of the assembled matrices afterwards.

    The idea: a decomposed covariance¶

    Each component's covariance is written through its eigendecomposition as

    \[ \Sigma_k=\lambda_k\,D_k\,A_k\,D_k^{\top},\qquad \det A_k=1,\quad D_k^{\top}D_k=I,\quad A_k=\operatorname{diag}(a_{k1}\ge\dots\ge a_{kd}>0). \]
    • \(\lambda_k=|\Sigma_k|^{1/d}\) (volume): a scalar controlling the component's overall size (its ellipsoid's volume scales as \(\lambda_k^{d/2}\)).
    • \(A_k\) (shape): a diagonal matrix with determinant 1 and decreasing entries -- how stretched the component is along its own axes.
    • \(D_k\) (orientation): an orthogonal matrix -- which way those axes point.

    The semi-axes of the one-standard-deviation ellipsoid are \(\sqrt{\lambda_k a_{kj}}\) along the \(j\)-th column of \(D_k\).

    Each factor can be **E**qual across components, **V**ary per component, or (for shape and orientation only) be the **I**dentity: \(A_k=I\) makes the component spherical, and \(D_k=I\) makes it axis-aligned (no rotation). Volume has no identity option -- it is always a free scalar, shared or not. The 14 valid combinations are named by their three-letter mclust code (Volume-Shape-Orientation); the last column is the number of free covariance parameters, which is what bic/aic charge for the covariances:

    Code tgmm name Meaning Free covariance parameters
    EII 'tied_spherical' one shared variance, spherical \(1\)
    VII 'spherical' per-component variance, spherical \(K\)
    EEI 'tied_diag' one shared diagonal covariance \(d\)
    VEI (new) per-component volume, shared diagonal shape \(K+(d-1)\)
    EVI (new) shared volume, per-component diagonal shape \(1+K(d-1)\)
    VVI 'diag' per-component diagonal covariance \(Kd\)
    EEE 'tied_full' one shared full covariance \(d(d+1)/2\)
    VEE (new) per-component volume, shared shape & orientation \(K+(d-1)+d(d-1)/2\)
    EVE (new) shared volume & orientation, per-component shape \(1+K(d-1)+d(d-1)/2\)
    EEV (new) shared volume & shape, per-component orientation \(d+K\,d(d-1)/2\)
    VVE (new) shared orientation, per-component volume & shape \(Kd+d(d-1)/2\)
    VEV (new) shared shape, per-component volume & orientation \(K+(d-1)+K\,d(d-1)/2\)
    EVV (new) shared volume, per-component shape & orientation \(1+K(d-1)+K\,d(d-1)/2\)
    VVV 'full' fully unconstrained per-component covariance \(K\,d(d+1)/2\)

    The counts are sums of three independent contributions:

    factor E V I
    volume \(\lambda\) \(1\) \(K\) --
    shape \(A\) (\(d-1\) free ratios) \(d-1\) \(K(d-1)\) \(0\)
    orientation \(D\) (\(d(d-1)/2\) free angles) \(d(d-1)/2\) \(K\,d(d-1)/2\) \(0\)

    The total number of parameters adds \(K-1\) free mixture weights and \(Kd\) mean coordinates. The 14 models are nested: a model is a special case of another if it is at least as constrained in all three factors (V before E before I), so \(\min J\) can only grow as constraints are added.

    Pass either name to covariance_type (mclust codes are case-insensitive):

    from tgmm import GaussianMixture

gmm = GaussianMixture(n_components=3, n_features=4, covariance_type='EVE')
gmm.fit(X)

print(gmm.covariance_type_)   # always the canonical mclust code, 'EVE' here
print(gmm.volume_, gmm.shape_, gmm.orientation_)  # the decomposition

    covariances_ is always the assembled (n_components, n_features, n_features) matrices regardless of which of the 14 codes you use -- predict/sample/score/plot_gmm/save/load all work unchanged. volume_/shape_/orientation_ expose the decomposition itself (None for the six classic types, which don't compute one). reg_covar is added to the assembled matrices, not to the decomposition, so the three factors describe the unregularised fit.

    Why bother with 8 more models?¶

    More constrained models (fewer free parameters) are less prone to overfitting on small or high-dimensional data, at the cost of flexibility. The 14-model family lets you search this trade-off systematically instead of jumping straight from "fully tied" to "fully free" -- which is exactly what select_model does by default:

    from tgmm import select_model

result = select_model(X, n_components=range(1, 8), criterion='bic')
print(result.best_.covariance_type_, result.best_.n_components)

    select_model's default covariance_types is all 14 GPCM codes (the mclust workflow); pass an explicit subset to narrow the search.

    How each model is estimated¶

    With the decomposition, \(|\Sigma_k|=\lambda_k^d\) and \(\Sigma_k^{-1}=\lambda_k^{-1}D_kA_k^{-1}D_k^{\top}\), so

    \[ J=\sum_{k}\Big[\,n_k\,d\log\lambda_k+\frac1{\lambda_k}\operatorname{tr}\big(A_k^{-1}D_k^{\top}W_kD_k\big)\Big]. \]

    Two lemmas do most of the work.

    Lemma 1 (shape)

    For symmetric positive-definite \(B\) and positive-definite \(C\) with \(\det C=1\): \(\operatorname{tr}(C^{-1}B)\ge d(\det B)^{1/d}\), with equality iff \(C=B/(\det B)^{1/d}\).

    Proof. The eigenvalues \(\mu_j\) of \(C^{-1/2}BC^{-1/2}\) multiply to \(\det B\). By AM-GM, \(\sum_j\mu_j\ge d(\prod_j\mu_j)^{1/d}\), with equality iff all \(\mu_j\) are equal, i.e. \(B\propto C\).

    Lemma 2 (orientation)

    For symmetric \(W\) with eigenvalues \(\omega_1\ge\dots\ge\omega_d\) and \(a_1\ge\dots\ge a_d>0\), over orthogonal \(D\): \(\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}D^{\top}WD)\ge\sum_j\omega_j/a_j\), attained when the columns of \(D\) are the eigenvectors of \(W\) in decreasing eigenvalue order. (The diagonal of \(D^{\top}WD\) is majorised by \(\omega\) -- Schur-Horn -- and increasing weights \(1/a_j\) are minimised by pairing the largest \(\omega\) with the largest \(a\).) This is why tgmm keeps shapes sorted in decreasing order.

    Closed-form models¶

    The six classic codes (EII, VII, EEI, VVI, EEE, VVV) have the closed forms of the Gaussian Mixture Model guide. Three of the new ones do too:

    Code Estimate
    EVI \(A_k=B_k/\lvert B_k\rvert^{1/d}\) with \(B_k=\operatorname{diag}(W_k)\); \(\lambda=\frac1n\sum_k\lvert B_k\rvert^{1/d}\)
    EEV \(D_k\) = eigenvectors of \(W_k\) (eigenvalues \(\Omega_k\)); with \(\Omega=\sum_k\Omega_k\): \(A=\Omega/\lvert\Omega\rvert^{1/d}\), \(\lambda=\lvert\Omega\rvert^{1/d}/n\)
    EVV \(C_k=D_kA_kD_k^{\top}=W_k/\lvert W_k\rvert^{1/d}\); \(\lambda=\frac1n\sum_k\lvert W_k\rvert^{1/d}\)

    (For EVI, only \(\operatorname{diag}(W_k)\) enters \(\operatorname{tr}(A_k^{-1}W_k)\); Lemma 1 gives \(A_k\) and \(J=nd\log\lambda+\frac d\lambda\sum_k|B_k|^{1/d}\), minimised at the stated \(\lambda\). EEV and EVV follow the same pattern, using Lemma 2 for EEV's orientations.)

    The iterative models: VEI, VEE, VEV¶

    With per-component volumes and a shared shape, the factors are coupled but each has a closed form given the other, so tgmm alternates:

    • given the shape: \(\lambda_k=\operatorname{tr}(A^{-1}D_k^{\top}W_kD_k)/(n_k d)\);
    • given the volumes: \(A=B/|B|^{1/d}\) (Lemma 1) with \(B=\sum_kD_k^{\top}W_kD_k/\lambda_k\) -- namely \(\operatorname{diag}(\sum_kW_k/\lambda_k)\) for VEI; \(\sum_kW_k/\lambda_k\) for VEE, where the shared matrix \(C=DAD^{\top}\) is updated as a whole; and \(\sum_k\Omega_k/\lambda_k\) for VEV, where each \(D_k\) is fixed once and for all to the eigenvectors of \(W_k\) (Lemma 2).

    Each sub-step is an exact minimiser, so \(J\) is non-increasing (coordinate descent).

    The two numerically-optimised models: EVE, VVE¶

    Given a shared orientation \(D\) everything else is closed-form: with \(B_k=\operatorname{diag}(D^{\top}W_kD)\),

    \[ A_k=\frac{B_k}{|B_k|^{1/d}},\qquad \lambda=\frac1n\sum_k|B_k|^{1/d}\ \ (\texttt{EVE}),\qquad \lambda_k=\frac{|B_k|^{1/d}}{n_k}\ \ (\texttt{VVE}). \]

    Given the shapes and volumes, the best \(D\) minimises \(\sum_k\lambda_k^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(A_k^{-1}D^{\top}W_kD)\) over orthogonal matrices -- the common principal components problem (Flury, 1984), which has no closed form for \(K>1\). (Profiling out \(\lambda_k\) and \(A_k\) from VVE gives Flury's criterion \(\sum_kn_k\log|\operatorname{diag}(D^{\top}S_kD)|\), \(S_k=W_k/n_k\), which by Hadamard's inequality is bounded below by the VVV optimum, with equality iff one \(D\) diagonalises every \(S_k\).) tgmm solves it with a few L-BFGS steps on the orthogonal manifold (PyTorch's orthogonal parametrisation), warm-started at the previous \(D\), and accepts the result only if the objective decreases -- a generalised EM step (Dempster, Laird & Rubin, 1977): \(Q\) is improved rather than exactly maximised, which keeps the log-likelihood monotone across EM iterations as for every other covariance type.

    Stopping and warm starts¶

    VEI, VEE, VEV, EVE and VVE repeat their inner alternation until the relative change in \(J\) falls below gpcm_tol (default 1e-6) or gpcm_max_iter (default 20) sweeps are done:

    gmm = GaussianMixture(n_components=3, n_features=4, covariance_type='VEE',
                       gpcm_tol=1e-8, gpcm_max_iter=50)

    Each EM iteration's alternation is warm-started from the previous EM iteration's decomposition, so in practice it usually takes only 1-2 sweeps to re-settle.

    Numerical verification¶

    The tutorial compares every code's mstep_gpcm objective value with an independent brute-force L-BFGS minimisation of \(J\) over each model's feasible set (log-volumes, mean-centred log-shapes for \(\det A=1\), orthogonal matrices from the matrix exponential of a skew-symmetric one): all 14 agree to optimiser tolerance, including the alternating and orthogonal-manifold models.

    What's and isn't supported for the 8 new codes¶

    • MLE only. mean_prior/covariance_prior (MAP/NIW estimation) raise NotImplementedError for the 8 new codes -- a conjugate-prior derivation specific to each parameterization is out of scope. full/diag/ spherical/tied_* still support MAP/NIW as before. weight_concentration_prior (the Dirichlet weight prior) is independent of the covariance parameterization and works with all 14.
    • No Gibbs mode. GaussianMixture(n_components=None, covariance_type=...) raises NotImplementedError for the 8 new codes, the same way it already does for tied_* -- a covariance that couples components (shared shape or orientation) doesn't factor per component the way Neal's Algorithm 3 needs.
    • init_covariances='empirical'/'global' project the usual empirical starting point onto the requested GPCM parameterization (the constrained MLE of that starting point); 'eye'/'random' just start from plain identity/random full matrices, since there's nothing to project yet.
