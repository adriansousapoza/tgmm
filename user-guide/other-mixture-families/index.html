    Other Mixture Families¶

    GaussianMixture assumes every component is normally distributed. That's the right assumption for a lot of data, but not all of it: outliers can drag a Gaussian component's covariance around, and directional data (unit vectors -- normalized embeddings, in practice) isn't naturally described by a density defined over all of \(\mathbb{R}^d\) in the first place. tgmm has two more mixture families for exactly those two cases, StudentTMixture and VonMisesFisherMixture, built on the same shared BaseMixture machinery as GaussianMixture -- the same fit/predict/ score/bic/aic/icl API, the same algorithm/temperature/ step_size options for the EM loop itself.

    Both families also support partial and full label information through fit(X, labels=...) (see Semi-Supervised and Positive-Unlabeled Fitting). Neither supports Gibbs-sampling mode (n_components=None), the 14 GPCM covariance codes, or MAP/NIW priors -- those stay GaussianMixture-only.

    Notation used on this page and throughout the user guide: \(n\) samples \(x_i \in \mathbb{R}^d\), \(K\) components, mixing weights \(\pi_k\), responsibilities \(r_{ik} = P(z_i = k \mid x_i)\), effective component counts \(n_k = \sum_i r_{ik}\).

    The shared machinery: one EM loop for every family¶

    Every mixture family in tgmm is a finite mixture

    \[ p(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \pi_k \, f_k(x \mid \theta_k), \qquad \ell(\theta) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log \sum_{k=1}^{K} \pi_k \, f_k(x_i \mid \theta_k), \]

    and BaseMixture implements the generic loop once. A family only supplies the component log-density \(\log f_k\) (_component_log_prob), the accumulation of its sufficient statistics (_compute_stats), and its M-step (_m_step_from_stats).

    E-step. The responsibilities are computed in log space (a softmax over components, so nothing under/overflows):

    \[ r_{ik} = \frac{\pi_k f_k(x_i)}{\sum_{j} \pi_j f_j(x_i)} = \exp\!\Big(\log \pi_k + \log f_k(x_i) - \operatorname{logsumexp}_j\big(\log \pi_j + \log f_j(x_i)\big)\Big). \]

    The per-sample normalizer \(\log \sum_j \pi_j f_j(x_i)\) is also what score_samples returns, and its mean is lower_bound_ (the average log-likelihood \(\ell(\theta)/n\) that tol is checked against).

    M-step. Each family maximizes (or, for Student-t, conditionally maximizes) the expected complete-data log-likelihood \(\sum_{i,k} r_{ik} \big[\log \pi_k + \log f_k(x_i)\big]\). The mixing weights are always

    \[ \pi_k = \frac{n_k}{n}. \]

    Variants of the loop, all shared by every family:

    algorithm What replaces the soft \(r_{ik}\) fed to the M-step
    'em' \(r_{ik}\) itself.
    'cem' One-hot \(\mathbb{1}[k = \arg\max_j r_{ij}]\) (Classification EM).
    'sem' One-hot of a sample \(z_i \sim \mathrm{Categorical}(r_{i\cdot})\) (Stochastic EM).
    'saem' A running average of sampled-assignment statistics with a decaying step size.

    temperature=(beta_0, n_anneal) (deterministic annealing) tempers the E-step: \(r_{ik} \propto \big(\pi_k f_k(x_i)\big)^{\beta}\) with \(\beta\) rising geometrically from beta_0 to \(1\) over the first n_anneal iterations, \(\beta_t = \beta_0^{\,1 - t/n_{\text{anneal}}}\). step_size switches to online/mini-batch EM, blending each batch's sufficient statistics into a running accumulator. See the Gaussian Mixture Model guide for when to use each.

    Model selection. All families share

    \[ \mathrm{BIC} = -2\,\ell(\hat\theta) + p \ln n, \qquad \mathrm{AIC} = -2\,\ell(\hat\theta) + 2p, \]
    \[ \mathrm{ICL} = \mathrm{BIC} + 2\,\mathrm{EN}(r), \qquad \mathrm{EN}(r) = -\sum_{i} \ln r_{i\hat z_i} \;\;(\texttt{mode='hard'}), \quad \text{or} \quad -\sum_{i,k} r_{ik}\ln r_{ik} \;\;(\texttt{mode='soft'}), \]

    (all "lower is better"), where \(p\) is the number of free parameters (_n_parameters(), family-specific; given in each section below) and \(\hat z_i = \arg\max_k r_{ik}\). The entropy term is \(\ge 0\) and vanishes when every responsibility is one-hot, so \(\mathrm{ICL} \ge \mathrm{BIC}\) with equality only for perfectly separated clusters. Criteria are directly comparable within a family at different \(K\) (or covariance structure), and between families whose densities share a base measure -- Gaussian and Student-t, both on \(\mathbb{R}^d\). They are not comparable between a vMF mixture (density on the sphere) and the other two.

    StudentTMixture: robust to outliers¶

    Why heavy tails help¶

    A multivariate Student-t distribution looks like a Gaussian near its center but has polynomially decaying tails, controlled by its degrees of freedom \(\nu\): small \(\nu\) means heavy tails (very forgiving of far-away points), and as \(\nu \to \infty\) it converges to a Gaussian exactly. A Gaussian component's covariance estimate is a plain average of squared deviations, so a single far-away point can inflate it arbitrarily; the t-component instead downweights such points automatically (derived below).

    Model¶

    Component \(k\) has mean \(\mu_k\), scale matrix \(\Sigma_k\) and degrees of freedom \(\nu_k\):

    \[ t(x \mid \mu_k, \Sigma_k, \nu_k) = \frac{\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{\nu_k + d}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\!\big(\tfrac{\nu_k}{2}\big)\,(\nu_k \pi)^{d/2}\,|\Sigma_k|^{1/2}} \Big(1 + \frac{\delta_{ik}}{\nu_k}\Big)^{-\frac{\nu_k + d}{2}}, \qquad \delta_{ik} = (x_i - \mu_k)^\top \Sigma_k^{-1} (x_i - \mu_k). \]

    This is exactly what _component_log_prob evaluates (and it matches scipy.stats.multivariate_t to machine precision). The key to fitting it is its scale-mixture representation: draw a latent precision multiplier \(w \sim \mathrm{Gamma}(\nu_k/2, \nu_k/2)\) (shape/rate, mean \(1\)), then \(x \mid w \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_k, \Sigma_k / w)\). Marginalizing \(w\) gives the t density above. (sample uses this representation directly: \(x = \mu_k + z/\sqrt{w}\) with \(z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_k)\).)

    Scale matrix vs. covariance

    covariances_ holds the scale matrix \(\Sigma_k\), not the covariance of the distribution. For \(\nu_k > 2\) the actual covariance is \(\frac{\nu_k}{\nu_k - 2}\,\Sigma_k\); for \(\nu_k \le 2\) it is infinite.

    E-step: responsibilities and robustness weights¶

    Besides the usual \(r_{ik}\), the latent multiplier has a closed-form posterior given \(x_i\) and \(z_i = k\): \(w_{ik} \mid x_i \sim \mathrm{Gamma}\!\big(\tfrac{\nu_k + d}{2}, \tfrac{\nu_k + \delta_{ik}}{2}\big)\), whose mean is

    \[ u_{ik} = \mathbb{E}[w_{ik} \mid x_i, z_i = k] = \frac{\nu_k + d}{\nu_k + \delta_{ik}}. \]

    Points near the center (\(\delta_{ik} \ll \nu_k\)) get \(u_{ik} \approx (\nu_k + d)/\nu_k > 1\); points far away (\(\delta_{ik} \gg \nu_k\)) get \(u_{ik} \to 0\). That is the robustness mechanism: \(u_{ik}\) is a per-sample, per-component weight that shrinks with Mahalanobis distance. sample_weights(X) returns \(u_{i\hat z_i}\) at each point's predicted component, so small values flag likely outliers.

    M-step (ECM)¶

    With \(m_k = \sum_i r_{ik} u_{ik}\), the updates are

    \[ \pi_k = \frac{n_k}{n}, \qquad \mu_k = \frac{\sum_i r_{ik} u_{ik}\, x_i}{m_k}, \qquad \Sigma_k = \frac{\sum_i r_{ik} u_{ik}\,(x_i - \mu_k)(x_i - \mu_k)^\top}{n_k} \;\;(+\, \texttt{reg\_covar}\cdot I). \]

    Compare with the Gaussian M-step: the only change is the extra weight \(u_{ik}\) inside the sums (and the mean's denominator being \(m_k\), not \(n_k\)). With \(\nu \to \infty\), \(u_{ik} \to 1\) and these reduce to the Gaussian updates (dof=1e4 is an easy sanity check against GaussianMixture). The statistics \(\mu_k\) and \(\Sigma_k\) are computed with \(u_{ik}\) evaluated at the previous parameters within the same iteration, which is what makes this an ECM (expectation conditional maximization) algorithm rather than plain EM: each parameter block is maximized conditionally on the others.

    For covariance_type='diag' the scatter and the denominator are the per-feature (diagonal) versions; 'spherical' uses \(\sigma_k^2 = \sum_i r_{ik} u_{ik}\,\lVert x_i - \mu_k\rVert^2 / (d\, n_k)\); 'tied_full' pools the scatter and the denominator across components, \(\Sigma = \sum_k \sum_i r_{ik} u_{ik} (x_i - \mu_k)(x_i - \mu_k)^\top \big/ \sum_k n_k\).

    scale_update='ecme' divides by \(m_k\) instead of \(n_k\). At the maximum-likelihood fixed point the two coincide: the stationarity condition for \(\Sigma_k\) gives \(\sum_i r_{ik} u_{ik} \delta_{ik} = d\, n_k\), and since \(u\,\delta = (\nu + d) - \nu\, u\) this forces \(m_k = n_k\). Both variants therefore converge to the same estimate; 'ecme' just takes a different path and often needs a few fewer iterations.

    Degrees of freedom¶

    For dof='per_component' each \(\nu_k\) solves a one-dimensional equation every M-step. Writing

    \[ c_k = \frac{1}{n_k}\sum_i r_{ik}\,\big(\ln u_{ik} - u_{ik}\big), \]

    tgmm solves

    \[ 1 - \psi\!\Big(\frac{\nu}{2}\Big) + \ln\!\Big(\frac{\nu}{2}\Big) + c_k + \psi\!\Big(\frac{\nu + d}{2}\Big) - \ln\!\Big(\frac{\nu + d}{2}\Big) = 0 \]

    for \(\nu\) (\(\psi\) the digamma function) by 60 bisection steps inside dof_bounds followed by 4 Newton steps on \(\ln\nu\), clamping to dof_bounds (default (0.1, 1000)) -- if the root lies outside the bounds the fit lands on the bound. This is the first-order condition of the expected complete-data log-likelihood in \(\nu\) (the Gamma\((\nu/2,\nu/2)\) part). For dof='shared', \(c\) is the pooled average over all components, \(\sum_k \sum_i r_{ik}(\ln u_{ik} - u_{ik}) / \sum_k n_k\), and one \(\nu\) is solved for. A numeric dof fixes every \(\nu_k\) for the whole fit.

    Where this deviates from the textbook

    The textbook update (Peel & McLachlan, 2000) evaluates the last two terms, \(\psi\big(\tfrac{\nu^{(t)} + d}{2}\big) - \ln\big(\tfrac{\nu^{(t)} + d}{2}\big)\), at the previous iterate \(\nu^{(t)}\). tgmm evaluates them at the candidate \(\nu\) itself. The two share the same fixed point (at convergence \(\nu^{(t)} = \nu\); the residual of the textbook equation at a converged tgmm fit is \(\sim 10^{-6}\)), so the final estimate is the same maximum-likelihood solution, but the per-iteration step is not literally the textbook EM step.

    Parameters, attributes, and counts¶

    Attribute Shape Meaning
    weights_ \((K,)\) \(\pi_k\)
    means_ \((K, d)\) \(\mu_k\)
    covariances_ by covariance_type scale matrix \(\Sigma_k\) (not the covariance)
    dof_ \((K,)\) fitted \(\nu_k\) (all entries equal for 'shared' or a fixed float)

    The number of free parameters used by bic/aic/icl is \(p = (K-1) + Kd + p_\Sigma + p_\nu\), with \(p_\Sigma = K\,d(d+1)/2\) (full), \(Kd\) (diag), \(K\) (spherical) or \(d(d+1)/2\) (tied_full), and \(p_\nu = K\) (per_component), \(1\) (shared) or \(0\) (fixed dof).

    Usage¶

    from tgmm import StudentTMixture

stm = StudentTMixture(n_components=3, covariance_type='full', dof='per_component')
stm.fit(X)

stm.means_        # (n_components, n_features)
stm.covariances_  # the scale matrix, not technically a covariance for finite dof
stm.dof_          # (n_components,) -- fitted nu_k
    • covariance_type: one of 'full', 'diag', 'spherical', 'tied_full' (the GPCM parameterizations and the Gibbs-only axis-aligned tied types aren't supported here).
    • dof: 'per_component' (default, one \(\nu_k\) per component), 'shared' (one \(\nu\) for every component), or a fixed float. dof_init (default 5.0) is the starting value, dof_bounds the clamp.
    • scale_update: 'ecm' (default) or 'ecme', see above.

    Flagging outliers with sample_weights¶

    After fitting, sample_weights(X) returns each point's \(u_{ik}\) at its predicted component -- small values flag points the model effectively treated as outliers:

    weights = stm.sample_weights(X)
likely_outliers = X[weights < 0.3]

    Caveats¶

    • A fitted \(\nu_k\) near dof_bounds[1] means that component is indistinguishable from a Gaussian; near dof_bounds[0] means a very heavy tail (infinite mean for \(\nu \le 1\)).
    • Each iteration needs the full \((n, K)\) matrix of Mahalanobis distances in memory (batch_size only chunks the E-step's reduction, not this).
    • Student-t and Gaussian densities share the same base measure (Lebesgue on \(\mathbb{R}^d\)), so a StudentTMixture's score, bic, aic and icl are directly comparable with those of a GaussianMixture fitted to the same data (the t pays for its extra \(\nu\) parameters in the count).

    VonMisesFisherMixture: directional data¶

    Why a different density¶

    For L2-normalized data -- points on the unit hypersphere \(\mathbb{S}^{d-1} = \{x : \lVert x\rVert = 1\}\), as you get from normalizing text or image embeddings before clustering by cosine similarity -- a Gaussian (or Student-t) mixture is the wrong model: it's defined over all of \(\mathbb{R}^d\) and has no notion of "direction only." The von Mises-Fisher (vMF) distribution is the natural analogue restricted to the sphere.

    Model¶

    Component \(k\) has a mean direction \(\mu_k\) (unit norm) and a concentration \(\kappa_k \ge 0\):

    \[ f(x \mid \mu_k, \kappa_k) = C_d(\kappa_k)\,\exp\!\big(\kappa_k\, \mu_k^\top x\big), \qquad C_d(\kappa) = \frac{\kappa^{d/2 - 1}}{(2\pi)^{d/2}\, I_{d/2 - 1}(\kappa)}, \]

    where \(I_\nu\) is the modified Bessel function of the first kind. The log-density tgmm evaluates is \(\log C_d(\kappa_k) + \kappa_k\, x^\top \mu_k\) (verified against scipy.stats.vonmises_fisher). The density depends on \(x\) only through the cosine similarity \(\mu_k^\top x\): higher \(\kappa_k\) is a tighter cluster around \(\mu_k\), and \(\kappa_k \to 0\) approaches the uniform distribution on the sphere. \(\log I_\nu(\kappa)\) is computed from scipy.special.ive (exponentially scaled, so it doesn't overflow), with a uniform large-order asymptotic expansion as a fallback when ive underflows -- which happens in high dimension at modest \(\kappa\).

    E-step and M-step¶

    The E-step is the generic one above with these \(f_k\). For the M-step, form the resultant vector of each component,

    \[ \mathbf{R}_k = \sum_{i} r_{ik}\, x_i, \qquad \bar R_k = \frac{\lVert \mathbf{R}_k \rVert}{n_k} \in [0, 1], \]

    (the length of the responsibility-weighted mean vector; \(\bar R_k\) near \(1\) means the points are nearly parallel). The maximum-likelihood updates are

    \[ \pi_k = \frac{n_k}{n}, \qquad \mu_k = \frac{\mathbf{R}_k}{\lVert \mathbf{R}_k \rVert}, \qquad A_d(\hat\kappa_k) = \bar R_k, \quad A_d(\kappa) = \frac{I_{d/2}(\kappa)}{I_{d/2 - 1}(\kappa)}. \]

    \(A_d(\kappa) = \bar R\) has no closed-form solution. tgmm follows Banerjee, Dhillon, Ghosh & Sra (2005): start from the closed-form approximation

    \[ \kappa^{(0)} = \frac{\bar R\,(d - \bar R^2)}{1 - \bar R^2}, \]

    then refine with two Newton steps on \(A_d(\kappa) - \bar R = 0\) using \(A_d'(\kappa) = 1 - A_d(\kappa)^2 - \frac{d-1}{\kappa} A_d(\kappa)\) (Sra, 2012), clamping \(\kappa\) to \([10^{-3}, 10^{5}]\). (The Banerjee approximation alone leaves a residual \(A_d(\kappa^{(0)}) - \bar R\) of up to \(\sim 10^{-2}\); after the two Newton steps the residual is \(\lesssim 10^{-7}\) in the cases we checked.) With concentration='shared', one \(\kappa\) is solved from the pooled resultant length \(\sum_k \lVert\mathbf{R}_k\rVert / \sum_k n_k\).

    Relation to spherical k-means

    With algorithm='cem', equal weights and a common \(\kappa\), the E/C-step assigns each point to the component of largest cosine similarity and the M-step sets each mean direction to the normalized sum of its members -- i.e. spherical k-means (Dhillon & Modha, 2001). tgmm's default init_means='kmeans' runs exactly that, seeded with a cosine-distance k-means++ scheme (the next center is drawn with probability proportional to \(1 - \max_{\text{chosen}} \cos\)) to avoid two initial centers landing in one true cluster.

    Parameters, attributes, and counts¶

    Attribute Shape Meaning
    weights_ \((K,)\) \(\pi_k\)
    means_ \((K, d)\) unit-norm mean directions \(\mu_k\)
    kappa_ \((K,)\) fitted concentrations \(\kappa_k\) (all equal for 'shared')

    Each \(\mu_k\) lives on a \((d-1)\)-sphere, so \(p = (K-1) + K(d-1) + p_\kappa\) with \(p_\kappa = K\) (per_component) or \(1\) (shared).

    Usage¶

    from tgmm import VonMisesFisherMixture

vmf = VonMisesFisherMixture(n_components=5, concentration='per_component')
vmf.fit(X_normalized)  # X_normalized rows must have unit L2 norm

vmf.means_   # (n_components, n_features), each row unit-norm
vmf.kappa_   # (n_components,) -- fitted concentration

    If X isn't already unit-norm, fit/predict/score all normalize it automatically and warn once per call -- but fitting on already-normalized data is still the right thing to do, since that's what the ambient dimensionality (n_features) and the density itself assume.

    • concentration: 'per_component' (default) or 'shared' (one \(\kappa\) for every component). kappa_init (default 10.0) is the starting value.
    • init_means: 'kmeans' (default, spherical k-means as above), 'random' (uniform on the sphere), 'points' (random rows of X), or a tensor (normalized and used directly). See the design doc for the seeding failure mode this fixes.
    • sample(n) uses Wood's (1994) rejection sampler. reg_covar is accepted for interface parity but unused (there is no covariance).

    Visualizing a vMF fit¶

    plot_gmm cannot draw data on a sphere, so tgmm.plotting has vMF-specific tools: plot_vmf draws a fitted model on the circle (\(d=2\)) or sphere (\(d=3\)) -- points, mean-direction arrows, and spherical caps holding a chosen probability mass of each component -- and plot_vmf_cosine compares, in any dimension, each component's histogram of angles to its mean direction with the fitted density \(f_{\kappa}(\theta)\propto e^{\kappa\cos\theta}\sin^{d-2}\theta\). See von Mises-Fisher mixtures in the plotting guide and the tutorial.

    Caveats¶

    • \(\kappa\) is clamped to \([10^{-3}, 10^{5}]\); an extremely tight cluster (or duplicate points, \(\bar R \to 1\)) lands on the upper bound.
    • The Bessel normalizer is evaluated per component in Python each E-step, which is cheap (\(K\) scalars) but not GPU-resident.
    • vMF has no notion of cluster shape -- every component is isotropic around its mean direction. For elongated clusters on the sphere, consider PCA-reducing and a GaussianMixture/StudentTMixture instead.

    Comparing against GaussianMixture on normalized data¶

    Nothing stops you from fitting GaussianMixture(covariance_type='diag') on the same L2-normalized data -- it's a reasonable baseline, just not one that respects the data living on a sphere. Do not compare their bic/ aic/icl: a vMF density is taken with respect to the surface measure of the sphere, a Gaussian density with respect to Lebesgue measure on \(\mathbb{R}^d\), so the log-likelihoods are not on the same scale (information criteria are comparable only among vMF fits). Compare across families with a cosine-based clustering metric or agreement with known labels instead; see notebook 17 for a worked example on high-dimensional embeddings.

    Supervised and semi-supervised fitting¶

    Because fit(X, labels=...) lives in BaseMixture, both families accept it: with every row labeled they fit one block of components per class directly, and with -1 marking unknown rows they run semi-supervised EM (components_per_class, unlabeled_weight, label_confidence, predict_class). The math and the options are covered in Semi-Supervised and Positive-Unlabeled Fitting.

    Noise, missing values and custom families¶

    noise=True works for all three families (the noise density is uniform in the data's bounding box, or on the sphere for vMF); missing='em' for GaussianMixture and StudentTMixture; frozen_components and sample_weight for all of them. To fit a family that tgmm does not ship, implement the three-method Component protocol and use GenericMixture. See Noise, Missing Values and Conditioning.

    Shared machinery summary¶

    bic/aic/icl work the same way on both classes as on GaussianMixture (see the formulas above). sample(n) draws from the fitted mixture for both: a Gaussian scaled by \(1/\sqrt{\mathrm{Gamma}(\nu_k/2, \nu_k/2)}\) for StudentTMixture, and Wood's (1994) rejection sampler for VonMisesFisherMixture. algorithm='cem'/'sem'/'saem', temperature (deterministic annealing), step_size (mini-batch/online EM, plus partial_fit), and n_init all work exactly as documented for GaussianMixture, since they're implemented once, in BaseMixture, and shared by every family.
